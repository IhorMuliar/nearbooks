# NEAR Books

NEAR Books rebuilds NEAR Intents' revenue, buyback and issuance figures from raw transfer records and archival RPC, checks the public claims about them, and publishes attested daily snapshots of NEAR mainnet to a mainnet contract, [`nearbooks.near`](https://nearblocks.io/address/nearbooks.near), that decides dated predictions.

Live dashboard: **https://ihormuliar.github.io/nearbooks/** (reads `nearbooks.near` in your browser and re-checks every snapshot).

Track: Investment Thesis; also Open Innovation.

**Thesis** (v1, 2026-10-21, [`THESIS.md`](THESIS.md)): NEAR Intents earns real and growing revenue, but it does not shrink NEAR's supply, and at today's volume it is far from offsetting dilution. Holding NEAR today is a bet on Intents growing several times over, not on scarcity. Six on-chain predictions score it. Two are decided before judging: P5 on 2026-11-05 and P6 on 2026-11-15.

What you will find:
- **An attested oracle on NEAR.** A pinned WASM worker runs in OutLayer's Intel TDX enclave, reads NEAR mainnet at the first block of a UTC day, and returns its output to the contract through OutLayer's yield/resume. Anyone can check the TDX quote with `contract/scripts/verify_attestation.py`.
- **A keeper agent with bounded spending.** `keeper/keeper.py` requests and resolves snapshots once a day. It holds only a function-call key limited to two contract methods, and it draws deposits from a prepaid budget under a daily cap that the contract enforces.
- **Public claims, checked.** The eight most widely shared claims about NEAR's buybacks and issuance are graded against the data in [`docs/claims.md`](docs/claims.md). Sources include the official dashboard, SVRN, Latent Equity, Grey Ledger, Bitwise and HSP-029. `make verify` fails if a verdict stops following from the data.
- **A reproducible pipeline.** Python standard library and committed raw data. It reconciles Intents revenue, buybacks and issuance with DefiLlama, revenue.near.org, HSP-029 and press figures. Each finding has a tolerance derived from the data.
- **Falsifiable on-chain predictions.** Each has a date and a rule, and only the attested snapshot of that date can decide it.
- **A live dashboard and an MCP server.** Both read the contract over RPC and re-check each snapshot's hashes, pin and transaction.

## How it works

```
 NEAR mainnet ── archival RPC ──> OutLayer TDX worker (sha256 pinned in the contract)
                                     │ output bytes via yield/resume
 keeper (function-call key) ─┐       ▼
 anyone (with deposit) ──────┴──> nearbooks.near (NEAR mainnet)
                                     ├─ attested snapshots: one per UTC day, first valid wins
                                     ├─ predictions: decided only by the snapshot of their date
                                     └─ worker pin: timelocked re-pins
 OutLayer attestation (TDX quote) ──> verify_attestation.py, dashboard (site/), MCP server (mcp/)

 FastNear, DefiLlama, archival RPC ──> pipeline/fetch*.py ──> data/snapshot-*/ ──> compute.py ──> verify.py
```

1. For a UTC date D, the keeper or anyone else asks the contract for a snapshot at the first mainnet block of D and of D minus 28 days.
2. The contract asks OutLayer to run the worker pinned for D. A re-pin waits out a timelock and never applies to a date that has already started.
3. In the enclave the worker refuses blocks that are not the first of their day. It reads total supply, the three Intents revenue wallets and an `intents.near` reserves check, and prints JSON.
4. The contract stores that output under D with its sha256 and derives net issuance and the revenue wallets' share of new supply.
5. A prediction resolves only from the attested snapshot of its resolve date. If the metric is missing, it is void.
6. The pipeline is separate from the contract: it recomputes every finding from committed raw data; `make data` refreshes the data.

Chain of custody and the checks behind "verified": `docs/attestation.md`.

## Trust assumptions and limits

- **The contract account still holds its full-access key.** With it the code can be redeployed and every rule bypassed, including the 24 h re-pin timelock. Deleting it, which freezes the code, is the last step before submission; `make verify-deploy` lists the account's keys. Owner powers: `docs/attestation.md`.
- **The TDX quote is checked off-chain.** The contract checks only that the bytes came from the configured OutLayer contract, for the pinned hash, answering this request. `verify_attestation.py` checks the quote; the dashboard and MCP server check hashes, pin and transaction, not the quote.
- **RPC without state proofs.** The worker reads mainnet over HTTPS JSON-RPC. `evidence_hash` commits to the raw responses, and a local run of the pinned worker reproduced it.
- **Some published figures are hand-transcribed.** revenue.near.org and press figures were typed into `pipeline/config.json` with their URLs, because the pages render client-side.
- **F1 is not independent of DefiLlama.** Both sum the same wallets. F1 shows that DefiLlama's number reproduces from raw transfers with no timing shift, not that the wallets are the right ones.
- **The keeper key can still spend.** If it leaks, it can spend at most the daily cap on snapshots and its gas allowance. Bounds and the refusals shown on testnet: `docs/deployment.md`.

Other limits (the break-even model, sample size, native NEAR only): `docs/findings.md`, section "Limits of the pipeline".
